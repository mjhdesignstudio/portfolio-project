My First Accidental Sale

Wait...what?!?

8/21/2026 by Melissa Hundley



This is a short story about how I sold a piece of calligraphy completely unintentionally.

I was working part-time at a local community college at the time and the one benefit I received was three credits tuition free each term. I already have a couple of college degrees, so I didn't think much of it until I learned that they offered calligraphy classes.

I figured why not...so I signed up for the first one. I had been hand-lettering things for a little bit at the time, but I never really knew how to layout a piece and as that was part of the cirriculum, I was sold!

Class started and I found myself really enjoying working with a calligraphy pen and ink (broad edge nib that you dip into ink). I seemed to be a natural at it - although I often would reply, 'this is the only class I'm taking, so I have lots of time to work on it at home.'


After the second class (there were a total of three - each teaching a different form of calligraphy; foundational, italic, and uncial), I found myself bored and started playing around with the italic calligraphy that I had just learned.

One Sunday morning before church, I was chatting with one of the ladies that was volunteering back in the tech booth (I was probably playing bass that weekend) and I mentioned that I had been taking calligraphy classes and was working on a little personal project involving the lyrics to the song 'Reckless Love.'

She asked to see a picture of it, and when she saw it she declared 'This is mine...when it is finished, I am going to buy it.'

I remember thinking...'you're crazy...this is just me messing around with stuff.' Turns out, the colors of the piece matched her home decor perfectly and she was adament that she wanted this artwork on her wall. So I finished it, matted it and framed it. When asked about a price, I had no idea...I'd only maybe sold one piece before and it was a custom piece...so I counted up what the supplies cost again and gave her that price plus a few dollars to say I made some money on it.


Clearly that wasn't a high enough price for her and she overpaid me. I couldn't understand it at the time, but it started me on a path (that would take years) to regularly selling my artwork.
